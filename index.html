A smaller trust surface

Less infrastructure to trust.

There is no account system and no directory. Optional supernodes help when your network needs them — they never hold your identity or your room keys.

✓

✓
Conversations stay sealed

Chat, voice, files, and video are encrypted end to end. A relay moves bytes it cannot read.

✓
Metadata, described honestly

The short answers

Frequently asked questions.

Deeper detail lives on the architecture page.

Can a supernode read my conversations?

No. Chat, voice, and files are sealed end to end, and supernodes never receive room keys. They do see routing metadata: peer and room IDs, membership, timing, and byte volumes.

Do I need a supernode at all?

Often not — two peers with open networks connect directly. A supernode is there for when that fails, and for shared rooms, so you do not need port forwarding to reach your people.

Can I make video calls yet?

Not as a finished feature. The camera and screen paths exist and are encrypted, but coverage outside Windows is incomplete. Voice, chat, rooms, and files are what to rely on today.

Can I use the games or pages in a normal browser?

No. They run inside the native client through d://, and are intentionally not exposed to the open web.

Was this called ConquerD?

Yes. The project is now DoubleSlash and new links are minted as d://. Nothing on the wire changed: existing conquerd:// invites still work, and your identity, peers, and saved rooms carry over.
