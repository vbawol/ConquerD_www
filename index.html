DoubleSlash keeps identity and room ownership with participants while optional supernodes help when networks or group features need them.

No accounts or central directory

Your Ed25519 identity is created locally. Meet people through signed, expiring invites instead of searchable profiles or platform accounts.

02

Direct when possible

Peer sessions prefer direct QUIC, then work down an ordered list of WebSocket candidates, then an optional supernode relay — all without changing the feature model.

03

Features stay modular

Chat, audio, files, rooms, portals, and games are negotiated capabilities with explicit trust tiers and quotas—not hidden side channels.

Privacy you can reason about

Your conversations are not a supernode feature.

Supernodes can assist with routing, membership, and ephemeral rooms. They are not identity authorities and do not receive room content keys.

✓

✓
Room definitions stay client-owned

Encrypted room definitions live with participants, not on the supernode.

✓

Where the project stands

Honest status, not a launch page.

DoubleSlash is pre-release software built in public. Here is the short version — see the architecture page for the full breakdown.

Working today

Shipped in the client

  • Local Ed25519 identity and signed d:// invites
  • Direct chat and voice, with a fallback to a temporary private room
  • Rooms for up to 32 people: voice, chat, and files, sealed end to end
  • Supernode clusters that keep a room alive if one node goes down
  • In-app portal pages and multiplayer games

In progress

Being built now

  • Video calling and screen sharing — encrypted and working, but not a finished feature yet
  • Camera capture beyond Windows still needs real-hardware validation
  • Platform binary signing per release channel

Not planned

Out of scope by design

  • Accounts, a user directory, or any discovery service
  • A supernode that can read room content or hold room keys
  • A public web surface — portal pages and games run only inside the native client
  • Telemetry, analytics, or tracking of any kind
  • Mandatory infrastructure: two reachable peers never need a supernode

Desktop only for now — Windows, macOS, and Linux. Supernode discovery stays invite-based on purpose. Builds come from the rolling nightly channel, which ships SHA-256 sidecars and an intentionally unsigned development manifest.

The short answers

Frequently asked questions.

Deeper technical detail lives on the architecture page.

Does DoubleSlash operate an account or identity server?

No. Identities are generated and stored locally. Optional supernodes can assist with relay, membership, rooms, and hosted features, but they are not identity authorities.

Can a supernode read room conversations?

No. Room chat bodies, Opus audio, and file chunks are end-to-end sealed. A supernode still sees routing metadata such as peer IDs, room IDs, membership, timing, signatures, and byte volumes.

Can I make video calls yet?

Not as a finished feature. Camera and screen paths exist and are end-to-end encrypted, but coverage and validation outside Windows are incomplete. Voice, chat, rooms, and files are what to rely on today.

How many people fit in a room, and what happens if a node goes down?

Up to 32 participants per room. A cluster of supernodes can keep a room alive even if one node goes down. Rooms live in supernode memory only — the authoritative definition stays on your device.

Can I use the portal or games in a normal web browser?

No. Portal pages and games run inside the native client through d:// and the authenticated identity QUIC path. The portal is intentionally not exposed as a public browser surface.

Why would I need access to a DoubleSlash supernode?

You may not — two peers with open firewalls connect directly over QUIC. When that is not possible, a supernode gives you connectivity without port forwarding. It is opaque to your content but still sees routing metadata such as peer IDs and timing.

What happens when a direct connection fails?

DoubleSlash works down an ordered list of fallbacks, ending with a chosen supernode's QUIC relay. Direct calls also have a temporary private-room fallback while a direct path keeps trying to connect.

How are downloads and updates verified?

Tagged releases use an Ed25519-signed release manifest plus archive hash verification. The rolling nightly channel uses SHA-256 sidecars and an intentionally unsigned development manifest.

Wasn't this called ConquerD?

Yes. The project is now DoubleSlash, and the invite and portal protocol is D:// — new links are minted as d://. Nothing on the wire changed: existing conquerd:// invites keep working, the client still registers the old scheme with your operating system, and identities, trusted peers, and saved rooms carry over. Some internal names (crates, the portal bridge, relay paths) intentionally still read conquerd so the network stays compatible.
